A single health bar from a 16-bit arcade fighting game HUD, drawn face-on: one long shallow
horizontal box, about eight times as wide as it is tall.
It is a heavy bevelled metal frame with a deep empty channel sunk into it. The channel is the slot
the game fills in later with the player's health, so paint the channel flat #FF00FF magenta -- the
identical magenta as the void outside the bar -- and keep the metal frame unbroken all the way around
the channel on every side: top, bottom, left and right. The channel is therefore a completely
enclosed hole sitting inside the frame. The channel must not run out to either end of the bar, and
must not touch the outside of the bar anywhere.
The channel is a plain empty rectangle. There is nothing inside it: no fill, no coloured bar, no
segments, no divisions, no notches.
The frame is thick warm gunmetal, with a brightly lit bevel along its top and left, a dark shadowed
bevel along its bottom and right, and one rivet in each of its four corners.

FRAMING:
One object only, alone, drawn face-on and flat to the camera with no perspective and no tilt, painted
over a completely flat #FF00FF magenta void. Do not paint scenery, a background, a character, a
portrait, or any second object into the magenta. Nothing casts a shadow onto the magenta.
Leave a clear band of magenta on all four sides: the object must not touch or run off any edge of
the canvas.
The magenta is one single uniform colour from edge to edge of the canvas, with no gradient, no
texture, no noise and no light falling on it. Do not put a decorative border, a CRT bezel, a
vignette, scanlines or a matte around the canvas.
STYLE: 16-bit SNES arcade fighting-game interface art, hand-painted, chunky readable shapes, hard cel
edges, flat blocks of colour, heavy bevels with a clearly lit edge and a clearly shadowed edge,
rivets and panel lines, no photographic detail, no 3D render, no airbrush gradient.
